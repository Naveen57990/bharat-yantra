# Bharat Yantra demo-video script

**Target length:** about 3 minutes 40 seconds. The official HSC page prefers a 3–5 minute MP4/MOV video at 720p or higher, no larger than 80 MB. Keep a screen recording of the live prototype ready before recording.

## 0:00–0:25 — Team and local problem

Introduce the team members and their roles. Then say:

> Bharat Yantra is a map-based planning prototype for a Mumbai ward. It helps a planner change a scenario, see the mapped locations involved, and compare possible actions. We are using F/S Ward as a provisional test area. This is a demonstration, not an official municipal system.

## 0:25–0:50 — The map

Open [the live prototype](https://bharat-yantra.vercel.app/), show the map and layer controls.

> The map brings together the ward boundary, mapped roads, buildings, facilities and other local layers. The sources have different dates and quality, so the app shows the map as a local source preview. The boundary and data reuse status are still under review.

## 0:50–1:35 — Rainfall scenario

Open Rainfall and set **150 mm** over **8 hours**. Run the scenario and show the ranked inspection areas on the map.

> This setting gives us a gross rainfall volume of about 1.44 million cubic metres over the provisional ward boundary, if the same rainfall depth is assumed everywhere. The map then ranks places for field inspection using an illustrative terrain-and-drainage rule. It does not predict flooded streets, water depth, or affected homes.

## 1:35–2:20 — Emergency and health access

Open Road closure, choose one named road, and run the hypothetical closure. Then open Facility access and show routes to mapped health features. If needed, use a test point inside the ward and show the map highlight.

> This compares connectivity in a mapped road graph. The closure is hypothetical. A route distance is not a travel-time estimate, and road nodes are not people. The health locations are community-mapped and their current operating status and capacity are not verified. A closure can also be included in the facility route calculation to explore a different path.

## 2:20–3:05 — Budget and action tracking

Open Budget planner, change the planning envelope or a unit rate, place a candidate work, then open the action tracker and show the mapped task/status control.

> The budget tool compares quantities and editable cost assumptions and places candidate locations on the map. It does not claim that one option is officially approved or will prevent a measured amount of flooding. The tracker turns a selected plan into review tasks; it is not an official work-order system.

## 3:05–3:40 — What the prototype proves and what comes next

Show the GitHub README or Judge guide, then close with:

> The prototype shows that ward evidence, what-if inputs and map-linked review actions can be tested in one workflow. The next stage is to confirm data rights and source quality, field-check facilities and routes, and compare any future flood model against independent observations before making stronger claims. Bharat Yantra is designed to support review and comparison, not replace the people responsible for public decisions.

## Recording checks

- Use a stable desktop browser and wait for the map to finish loading before recording.
- Keep the cursor away from labels while speaking; zoom only when pointing to a specific result.
- Show inputs before outputs so judges can reproduce the scenario.
- Keep the limitation statement visible or spoken; do not call the shortlist a flood prediction.
- Export MP4 or MOV, at least 720p, 3–5 minutes, no larger than 80 MB.
- Verify the final file plays and upload it to the MY Bharat portal.
